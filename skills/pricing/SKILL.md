# Pricing

## The one rule

**Sell outcomes, at the moment of value, with numbers that are true.**

- **Outcomes**: every plan says what the buyer can finish ("≈ 40 invoices a month"), not only units ("200 credits").
- **Moment of value**: the upgrade appears right after the user got a result and wants more — continue, export, higher quality, more volume — not only when credits hit zero.
- **True**: every "save X%", original price, deadline and scarcity claim is arithmetically and factually real. Compute it and show the computation.

## Mode

Produce a proposal. Changing live prices or existing subscriptions needs explicit authorization plus a decision on grandfathering current customers.

## Decide in this order, in writing

1. **Buyer and value**: who pays, for which job, what they use today instead, evidence of willingness to pay, how often they need it.
2. **Billing model**: subscription, usage or one-time — whichever matches that frequency.
3. **Value metric**: what the buyer's value grows with (results, projects, seats, minutes, exports). Credits are a fallback and are always translated into outcomes.
4. **Cost and margin**: cost per core action → typical and heavy user per month → margin per plan. Aim for 80% or more where cost is material; say so when it is negligible.
5. **Plans**: the fewest plans that serve genuinely different buyers; volume alone can justify a tier. One plan is the natural default. The user's reference entry price is about $9.99 unless value evidence says otherwise; avoid launching cheap.
6. **Free**: a trial that reaches the first value moment, not a plan to live on. Default: no Free column in the pricing table.
7. **Annual**: only when value repeats over a year. Show the yearly total, the per-month equivalent and the real saving in $ and %; annual visually recommended, monthly one click away. Saving % = 1 − annual ÷ (monthly × 12).
8. **Value moments**: 3–5 points in the real product flow. For each: eligible user state (new, activated, power, paid — paid users excluded from walls they already passed), the offer form (result upgrade, inline, export wall, soft or hard wall), frequency cap, dismissal and return path.
9. **Checkout terms**: renewal, cancellation, refund and fulfilment statements match what the product and provider actually do.

## Persuasion that stays honest

Anchor with a real higher tier, mark one recommended option, frame value in outcomes, frame loss on real loss (work in progress, remaining quota). Countdown timers, invented scarcity and made-up original prices are removed wherever found.

## Output

Buyer/value notes · billing model and value metric with the rejected alternative · cost table · plan table (monthly, annual, computed saving, outcome per month, who) · value-moment map · dishonest elements found, each with its fix · assumptions to validate with real data.

When implementing, use the existing design system (DESIGN.md if present), then complete a sandbox purchase of the chosen plan and period and confirm the entitlement arrives and the user returns to their interrupted task, on desktop and mobile.
